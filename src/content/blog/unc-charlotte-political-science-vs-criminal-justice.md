If you are choosing between Political Science and Criminal Justice at UNC Charlotte and want to know which one buries you in science labs, the answer is short: **neither, and they are identical**. Both require seven credits of natural science and exactly one laboratory course, and every bit of that comes from the Charlotte Core — the University's general education program — not from either major.

Below is where that requirement actually lives, how Charlotte's lab courses are structured, which options are worth picking, and the one path where serious lab science does enter the picture.

Everything here is tied to a catalog year, and catalog years change. Confirm your own requirements in DegreeWorks before you register. Sources are linked at the bottom.

## The short answer

| | Political Science, B.A. | Criminal Justice, B.A. |
|---|---|---|
| Total credit hours | 120 | 120 |
| Hours in the major | 30 | 40 |
| Natural science required by the major | None | None |
| Natural science from the Charlotte Core | 7 credits | 7 credits |
| Lab courses required | Exactly one | Exactly one |
| Statistics required by the major | Methods course (POLS 2220) | STAT 1220, 1221, or 1222 |
| College | CHESS | CHESS |
| Language requirement | Through 1202 | Through 1202 |
| Delivery | On-campus and online | On-campus and online |

The science line is the same on both sides. If you are picking a major to dodge a lab, you are solving a problem that does not exist — and if you are picking one hoping for more science, neither will give it to you.

## Where the science requirement actually comes from

UNC Charlotte's general education program, the **Charlotte Core**, applies to every undergraduate regardless of major. Beginning Fall 2023 it requires ten courses totaling 31–32 credits:

- **First-Year Writing** (3–4 credits): WRDS 1103, or WRDS 1104 with the writing studio
- **Critical Thinking and Communication** (3 credits): CTCM 2530
- **Quantitative and Data** (6 credits): math, statistics, deductive logic, or computer science, of which at least 3 credits must carry a MATH or STAT prefix
- **Natural Sciences** (7 credits)
- **Themes** (9 credits): Global and Local theme courses
- **Foundations of American Democracy** (3 credits)

Neither the Political Science major nor the Criminal Justice major adds a science course on top of this. There is no chemistry, biology, or physics anywhere in the CJUS or POLS course lists.

One wrinkle worth knowing: the Provost's General Education policy page still describes Global and Local Themes as 12 credits, while University College's Charlotte Core page describes 9 credits of Themes plus the 3-credit Foundations of American Democracy requirement, which was added by policy revision in August 2026. The University College math is what adds up to the stated 31–32 total. DegreeWorks is the tiebreaker for your catalog year.

## The natural sciences requirement, exactly

The policy language is specific:

> Students must take two courses from the list below, one of which must be taken with its corresponding laboratory (L) course, for a total of 7 credits in the natural sciences.

So: two science courses, **one lab, not two**. The usual arithmetic is a 3-credit lecture, plus its 1-credit lab, plus a second 3-credit science course — seven credits total. Some combinations land at eight.

### How labs are packaged at Charlotte

This is the part that trips people up at registration, because Charlotte uses three different patterns:

**Separate 1-credit "L" course.** Most options work this way — BIOL 1110 and BIOL 1110L, CHEM 1251 and CHEM 1251L, GEOL 1200 and GEOL 1200L, PHYS 1130 and PHYS 1130L, and so on. The lab is its own registration. Enroll in the lecture and forget the L section and you will have a science course that does not satisfy the lab half of the requirement.

**Zero-credit lab attached to a 4-credit course.** ANTH 2141 is 4 credits with ANTH 2141L listed at 0 credits; ITIS 1350 and ITIS 1350L work the same way. You still register for the L section — it just carries no separate credit.

**Fully bundled.** BINF 1101 (4 credits) and GEOG 1103 (4 credits) include both lecture and lab in a single course. Nothing extra to add.

**Some courses have no lab at all.** BIOL 1115, CHEM 1200, and PHYS 1202 appear on the list with no corresponding L course. These can only serve as your second, non-lab science.

### Choosing your one lab

The approved list is broad, and a Political Science or Criminal Justice major has no reason to take the pre-med sequence. A few things worth knowing:

- **PSYC 1101 – General Psychology, with PSYC 1101L, counts as a natural science at UNC Charlotte.** At most universities psychology is a social science and will not fill a natural-science-with-lab slot. At Charlotte it is on the approved list, lab and all. For a social science major this is often the single most comfortable way to clear the requirement.
- **Earth and space options** — GEOL 1200 Physical Geology, GEOL 1210 Earth History, ESCI 1101 Earth Sciences-Geography, METR 1102 Introduction to Meteorology, PHYS 1130 Introduction to Astronomy — all have 1-credit labs and are designed for non-majors.
- **The unusual physics courses** — PHYS 1100 Conceptual Physics, PHYS 1201 Sports and Physics, PHYS 1203 Physics of Music — are real labs without the calculus-track workload.
- **The bundled 4-credit courses** — GEOG 1103 Spatial Thinking and BINF 1101 Introduction to Bioinformatics and Genomics — remove the separate-lab-registration failure mode entirely, which is worth something if your schedule is tight.
- **CHEM 1251/1252 General Chemistry** is on the list, but it exists for science majors and pre-health students. Take it only if you actually need it for a second major or for graduate plans, not to satisfy gen ed.

One scheduling note that matters more for Criminal Justice majors: lab sections meet in long blocks and are heavily clustered in afternoons. If you plan to do CJUS 3400 (Criminal Justice Internship) or a field placement, clear your lab in a semester when your afternoons are free.

## Where the two majors actually differ

The science requirement is a tie, so decide on everything else.

**Criminal Justice, B.A.** requires 40 hours of CJUS coursework within the 120-hour degree. The core is CJUS 1511 (Local Social Science: Foundations of Criminal Justice, formerly CJUS 1100), CJUS 1200 (Professionalism in Criminal Justice and Criminology), CJUS 2340 (Criminological Theory), and CJUS 2370 (Research Methods in Criminal Justice), plus STAT 1220, 1221, or 1222, one CJUS Crime Analytics course, one Engagement Across Perspectives course, and 21 hours of CJUS electives.

A few rules specific to CJUS that matter more than the science question:

- To declare the major you need CJUS 1511 with a **C or better** and a 2.0 GPA.
- CJUS 1511, 2340, 2370, and statistics must all be passed with a C or better.
- You may attempt CJUS 1511 and CJUS 2340 **no more than twice** — withdrawals and grade replacements count as attempts, and a third enrollment is blocked.
- CJUS 1511 is a prerequisite for nearly all other CJUS coursework, so take it early.

**Political Science, B.A.** requires 30 hours: six required courses plus at least four POLS electives. The required core spans American politics, comparative politics, international politics, political theory, methods (POLS 2220, Political Science Methods), and a senior seminar. There is also a Security and Intelligence Studies concentration for students aiming at national security work.

Note that Political Science renumbered its introductory courses to map onto the Charlotte Core, so you will see POLS 1501 (Global Social Science: Introduction to Comparative Politics), POLS 1511 (Local Social Science: Introduction to American Politics), and POLS 1575 (Foundations of American Democracy) alongside older numbers like POLS 1110 in earlier catalogs. Which six count for you depends on your catalog year — verify in DegreeWorks rather than trusting any course number you read secondhand, this article included.

**Both majors sit in CHESS**, the College of Humanities & Earth and Social Sciences, so both carry the same language requirement: proficiency through the **1202 level**. You can satisfy it with coursework at Charlotte, transfer or transient credit, AP or IB credit, CLEP, placement above 1202 on the placement test, or ASL credit from another institution. For most students this is a bigger scheduling item than the lab is.

### The double-counting nobody mentions

Both departments renumbered their introductory courses into Charlotte Core categories, which means your first major course is also a general education course:

- **CJUS 1511** is a Local Social Science theme course. Criminal Justice majors clear a Themes slot with a required major course.
- **POLS 1501** is a Global Social Science theme, **POLS 1511** is a Local Social Science theme, and **POLS 1575** satisfies Foundations of American Democracy outright.
- The **statistics course CJUS requires** also supplies 3 of your 6 Quantitative and Data credits, and satisfies the MATH-or-STAT portion.

Map these overlaps before you register. Students routinely take a standalone theme course they did not need because nobody told them their major course already counted.

## The other kind of lab

"Lab" at Charlotte does not only mean a bench and safety goggles.

The Department of Political Science and Public Administration runs **POLS-Lab**, the Public Opinion, Learning, and Sentiment Lab — a research facility for survey experiments and focus groups in public opinion, political and economic behavior, public policy, and social psychology. It occupies Fretwell 421, a 22-station computer lab with screen-capture software and headphones, ten stations equipped for video recording, a system for remote viewing of focus groups, and a biometric station with galvanic skin response sensors and software that codes facial movement to measure emotion. Subjects come largely from a student pool managed through SONA Systems, and the lab explicitly welcomes undergraduate researchers.

Criminal Justice has its own quantitative equivalent in the Crime Analytics sequence — CJUS 4370 Data Analytics and Crime, CJUS 4374 Geospatial Analytics and Crime, CJUS 4376 Social Network Analysis, CJUS 4377 Crime Measurement and Data Visualization, and others, with a Crime Analytics minor available.

If "lab experience" means research skills you can put on a résumé, this is where you get it in either major — and it will do more for your applications than a second semester of chemistry would.

## If you actually want forensics

This is where the real answer to the science question lives, and it is worth being blunt: **UNC Charlotte does not offer a forensic science major.** There is no forensics track inside the Criminal Justice degree either — the CJUS course list runs toward theory, law, corrections, policing, and data analytics, not toward laboratory work.

UNC Charlotte's own University Career Center, in its guidance for Criminal Justice majors, spells out the path:

> for a career in forensics—double major in biology or chemistry, complete an internship in a crime lab, and earn a master's degree in forensic science

That is the honest version. Crime laboratories hire from chemistry and biology backgrounds, and a Criminal Justice degree on its own does not qualify you for bench work. If that is your goal, the science is not one gen ed lab — it is a full second major, and you need to start the chemistry sequence early because CHEM 1251 gates everything after it. Decide this in your first year, not your third.

## Your checklist

1. **Run a DegreeWorks what-if audit** for both majors. It shows the real boxes and what you have already filled.
2. **Separate Charlotte Core from major requirements** on paper. Almost every mistake here comes from confusing the two.
3. **Check your catalog year.** Charlotte Core changed substantially in Fall 2023, and Foundations of American Democracy was added by policy revision in August 2026.
4. **Register the L section.** If your science course has a separate laboratory course, it is a separate registration.
5. **Map your double-counts** — CJUS 1511 or POLS 1501/1511/1575 against Themes and Foundations of American Democracy, and your statistics course against Quantitative and Data.
6. **Handle the 1202 language requirement early**, and check whether high school coursework, AP, IB, CLEP, or placement already cleared it.
7. **Check transfer exemptions.** Students transferring 60+ credit hours, or who met the requirement at another UNC System campus or an NC community college, are exempted from Foundations of American Democracy. An A.A., A.S., or A.F.A. can also affect the language requirement, and an A.A.S. in a criminal justice discipline can bring up to 15 hours of CJUS credit.
8. **Talk to the right advisor.** Criminal Justice advising runs through CONNECT and cjusadvisor@charlotte.edu. Political Science advising is Alison Mackie (amackie1@charlotte.edu), with Dr. Amber R. Knight as Associate Chair and Undergraduate Coordinator.

## Bottom line

At UNC Charlotte, science and labs are not a reason to pick one of these majors over the other. Seven credits, one lab, same list, same college, same language requirement. Choose on the actual content — 30 hours of political science with a senior seminar and a methods course, versus 40 hours of criminal justice with a required statistics course, a crime analytics component, and stricter progression rules — and on where you want to work afterward. Law school is open from either.

Then take the science requirement seriously exactly once, when you pick your lab. Choose a course you will enjoy at 2 p.m. on a Thursday, register the L section, and get on with your degree.
